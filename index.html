How it works

Step 1 of 4

A DNA nanostructure, designed in silico.

Its size and shape are controlled at the nanometer scale.

Step 2 of 4

Detection antibodies attach.

The structure works as a molecular adapter for the antibodies of a rapid test.

Step 3 of 4

A defined number of signal labels assemble.

An analyte molecule binding to the detection antibody is now labeled with multiple nanoparticles (instead of one in a conventional assay).

Step 4 of 4

Same rapid test. Up to 100× more sensitive.1

Lab-grade sensitivity. Rapid-test simplicity.

Illustration based on our structural model: DNA origami (blue), detection antibodies, and gold nanoparticles as signal labels. Test strips and test-line close-ups are schematic.

About us

Amplifold brings laboratory-grade sensitivity to rapid tests. Our DNA origami technology, developed at Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München (LMU), uses precisely engineered nanostructures to amplify the signal of lateral flow assays by up to two orders of magnitude, while keeping the speed and simplicity of a familiar test strip. Our first application, in development, is high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I testing at the point of care; infectious diseases are next.

Based in Munich and operating under a quality management system according to ISO 13485, Amplifold combines pioneers in DNA nanotechnology with diagnostics entrepreneurs who have built and sold a molecular diagnostics company before. We are backed by Matterwave Ventures, XISTA Science Ventures, Bayern Kapital, b2venture and Labor Becker.
